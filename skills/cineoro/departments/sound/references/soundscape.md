# Soundscape library

**Contents:** how to write a sound list · worlds · sound of behaviour · silence design · rhythmic
sounds that must stay diegetic · scores (when the film has one) · audio artifacts

---

## How to write a sound list

Order by prominence; give each source a behaviour and, when it drives action, a second.
```
SOUND — DIEGETIC ONLY, dirty-real: [dominant bed] ; [secondary bed] ; [the sounds the hands make] ;
[breath and body] ; [one off-screen event at Ns] ; [silence moment].
```
Avoid generic words ("ambient sounds", "atmospheric audio") — they are an invitation to a pad.

## Worlds (starting points — make them specific)

- **Polar / ice:** low wind across open ice; spindrift hissing; fine snow ticking on hide; the dry
  creak of frozen leather; runners grinding on crust; breath through teeth; a dog shifting its paws.
- **Fire night:** the fire's roar and spit; embers snapping in wind; wood settling; a distant dog;
  murmur of a crowd behind the camera; boots in trampled snow.
- **Interior by a small flame:** the wet flutter of the flame; wind outside the wall; skins ticking;
  breath close to the microphone; fabric shifting.
- **Rural house, morning:** a rooster far off; a kettle beginning to tick; a wooden chair; a spoon
  on enamel; flies; wind in a loose roof sheet.
- **City night:** distant traffic wash; a bus braking two streets away; a shop's fluorescent buzz; a
  scooter passing; footsteps on wet pavement; a window above with a TV murmur.
- **Forest:** leaves overhead in wind; a branch knocking; insects; boots on wet leaves; a bird alarm
  call when the character moves.
- **Sea / boat:** hull slap; rope creaking on a cleat; engine idle; gulls far; spray hitting a jacket.

## The sound of behaviour

The sounds the hands and bodies make are as convincing as the picture of them: the snap as a knot
seats, the rasp of a knife on a whetstone, the grain of a match, fabric dragging, a breath held during
effort and released after. Write them where the action is, with seconds when they matter.

## Silence design

- Decide where the sound drops away and how long the hold lasts: "after her line, only wind for two
  seconds".
- A sudden event can kill the bed: "a single huge low drum beat out of the dark; every distant voice
  dies instantly — only fire and wind".
- The heaviest moments get more silence, not more sound.

## Rhythmic sounds that must not become music

Drums, footsteps, heartbeats, machines and chanting turn into a score unless held down:
"a single huge hollow low BOOM out of the dark… BOOM, closer… diegetic, slow, spaced — NOT music,
NOT a rhythm pattern, NO score". Name the spacing, the source, the distance.

## When the film has music

- **Score:** describe it like a cue sheet — instrumentation, entry second, level under dialogue, how it
  builds, where it stops ("solo cello enters at 18s, very low, under the breath; strings join at 22s;
  cut dead at the hard end"). Generic "epic music" is replaced by the engine's stock score.
- **Source music:** a radio, a band in the room, a singer — place it in the world and give it a
  distance and an acoustic.
- **Music video / the user's track:** attach the song as an audio reference with the job "the only
  music; the performer lip-syncs the line '…' at [s–s]"; map the timeline onto the song's beats
  (downbeats, the drop, the chorus entry) and say "no other music, no added instruments".
- **Regional or period music:** name the instruments that belong and fence the ones that don't.

## Audio artifacts (official notes + production)

- **Bubbling / echo artifacts** → remove water, wave and echo wording from the prompt.
- **Music despite the ban** → repeat the ban with synonyms at the top and the end; strip in post if
  needed.
- **Metallic ringing, chimes, clean digital sheen** → name them in the negative; ask for "dirty-real
  location sound".
- **Click at the end of a clip** → fade in post.
- Generated audio is mono; plan stereo and mix in post.
