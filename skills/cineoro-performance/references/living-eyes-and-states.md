# Living eyes, altered states, crowds, animals

Read when a face is at high risk of the glassy stare, when a character is in an altered state, when
the frame holds a crowd, or when an animal must read as real.

**Contents:** why eyes die · the liveness line · risk ladder · altered states · tears, laughter,
screams · crowds · animals · children · eye-hiding devices

---

## Why AI eyes die

The engine renders a beautiful eye and then *forgets to move it*. The tell is a **frozen pupil**: no
saccades, no settling, a blink that arrives on a metronome or not at all, both halves of the face
moving as one. A highlight of fire or stars in the eye is not the problem — it is good. The fix is
movement with a cause.

## The liveness line (paste, then adapt the involuntary detail)

```
LIVING EYES — @NAME: the pupils move with the task — they search, settle, drift and settle again;
blink cadence uneven and a beat late; brows, cheeks and mouth work independently, slight asymmetry.
Involuntary detail: [ONE: a held breath / a swallow that cuts the phrase / a late blink / the jaw
setting once / a nostril flare on the inhale / the lower lid tightening]. The [light source]
reflects as a small live point in each eye — the reflection is fine, a FROZEN PUPIL is not.
```

For a listener: "eyes on his mouth, then to his eyes, then away to the floor and back — checking
whether he means it."

## Risk ladder — how much protection a face needs

| Situation | Risk | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| Wide, face < 10% of frame | low | task in one line, no liveness line needed |
| Medium, face readable | medium | ACTING short form + liveness line |
| Close-up / extreme close-up, holding | high | full ACTING block + liveness line + one involuntary detail + "never into the lens" |
| Close-up of a character who is absent, drugged, dying, mad | very high | all of the above + active-eye state description; or hide the eyes and play mouth/jaw/breath |
| Multiple faces in close two-shot | high | separate ACTING per face + "both faces stay individually readable and never blend or swap features" |

## Altered states — readable markers

Write the marker the camera can see, never the label.

- **Madness / psychosis:** eyes ACTIVE — darting, rolling, or wide with visible sclera and a fine
  tremor; dilated pupils; broken breathing; indistinct muttering; sweat; micro-expressions that never
  settle. Or the "absent competence" mode: the body performs a learned skill perfectly in the wrong
  world. Never "maniacal", "crazy eyes", "villain laugh".
- **Absence / dementia / dissociation:** eyes open, soft, unfocused but alive, aimed past the partner
  (over her head, into the dark); one slow late blink; faint pleasant slackness in the mouth; does not
  track people who leave.
- **Fever / exhaustion:** heavy lids that close for half a second too long; head lolls then corrects;
  hands miss and retry; breath shallow and quick.
- **Drunk:** over-precise gestures, a lag between deciding and doing, gaze that lands slightly beside
  the target and corrects.
- **Fear:** the body goes still first (animal freeze), breath stops, then fast shallow breathing;
  eyes widen with visible sclera and dart between the threat and the exit.
- **Grief held:** see "plays holding still" (acting-task.md); the eyes stay on the task; the break
  happens in breath and swallowing, not on the face.
- **Lucid moment inside madness:** the eyes find focus and *stay* — the stillness of the pupil is the
  event, made readable by contrast with the previous restlessness.

## Tears, laughter, screams

- **Tears** swell at the lid, gather, run along the side of the nose, catch on the lip; in cold they
  freeze on lashes and cheek. If two characters cry, write it for both. The nose runs.
- **Crying speech:** the break lives in the breath, in a swallow cutting a phrase in half, in a note
  going wet and closing in the throat (voice side: `cineoro-sound`).
- **Laughter:** breath-driven, hitching, restarting, the shoulders and belly involved; under tension
  short, wrong, swallowed. Never "maniacal cackle".
- **Screams:** ugly, hoarse, shapeless, the voice cracking and running out of air — never a clean
  cinematic wail. The body throws the scream forward from the belly.

## Crowds

A crowd is a single organism with a task, not a list of extras.
- Give the crowd one verb ("a grim jury, weighing", "a market ignoring her") and weight shifting,
  breath fog, small private actions (adjusting a hood, whispering to a neighbour).
- **Nobody in the crowd looks into the lens.** Reaction lag: the back rows react a beat after the
  front.
- Every face is distinct from every other and from the leads; the lead's face exists once
  (anti-clone lock — hand to `cineoro-space`).
- Background faces fall into darkness and out of focus; sharp faces only where the story needs them.

## Animals

- Real animal behaviour, specific to species and situation: ears, nostrils, weight on the paws, the
  tail as a meter. A working animal works; a wild animal keeps distance and an exit.
- The animal never looks knowingly into the lens, never glows, never behaves like a pet unless it is
  one.
- An animal's "tactic": fight while it can, hold the line, watch the handler, protect the young.
- Dying animal: the fight continues as long as the body allows — gradual, not a switch-off.

## Children

Children don't perform emotions; they do concrete things with total commitment (count stones, guard
a toy, copy an adult's gesture a beat late). Write the activity and the distraction.

## Eye-hiding devices (when the risk is too high)

Snow goggles or sunglasses, a deep hood shadow, a hat brim, smoke between lens and face, hair across
the eyes, a backlit silhouette, a profile turned away, hands over the face, a mask pushed down. Then
the performance runs through the **mouth, the jaw and the breath**. If the face must stay hidden,
say "FACE NOT SHOWN" in every block that could reveal it (hand to `cineoro-realism`).
