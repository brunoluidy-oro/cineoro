# Behaviour mechanics — the library

A viewer does not audit skin; they watch what the hands do. If the hands do something invented,
nothing else survives. Every physical action is written as **mechanics with resistance**: the
material answers back, attempts fail and are retried, weight goes somewhere, the result is tested.

**Contents:** the rule · the five-part action sentence · walking and carrying · hand work · craft
processes · contact between people · cold, heat, wet · the "full process" principle

---

## The rule

Movement is described as mechanics, never as a word.
- Not "she walks" → "she hauls a loaded sled over wind-packed snow: torso folded forward, the strap
  cutting into her shoulder, two or three steps, the runners catching on the crust, and she leans in
  again."
- Not "he lies there" → "he lies lashed down, covered to the throat, and his body follows every pull
  of hers as dead weight."

## The five-part action sentence

For any action that matters, write:
1. **Start state** (already in progress at the first frame, if possible).
2. **Resistance** — what the material/body/person does back (stiff, slippery, heavy, springing back).
3. **Attempts** — how many, and what changes between them.
4. **Result** — the moment it gives (seats, snaps, catches, opens) — sudden, not smooth.
5. **Test / after** — the check, the wipe of the hand, the next small task.

## Walking and carrying

- **Walking** — heel contact → weight transfer → hip shift → toe push-off → mass settling. Surface
  changes the walk: wind-packed snow (short steps, feet testing), mud (suction, the boot pulls
  free), sand (heels sink), wet stone (flat feet, arms out), stairs (hand on the rail).
- **Hauling / dragging** — torso folded forward, the line taut, stop-start rhythm, the load catching
  and breaking loose with a jolt.
- **Carrying weight** — the body counter-leans, the free arm out for balance, shifts the load between
  steps, short breath.
- **Being dragged / dead weight** — limbs follow late, the head lolls, cloth bunches at the pull point.
- **Running** — real ground contact, knee lift, opposing arm swing, torso lean, varied stride,
  breath audible.

## Hand work

- **Knots / rope / straps** — turn around the post, thread back, first knot jerked down hard, second
  laid over, hauled tight with two pulls, test tugs. Stiff material springs back.
- **Knife / tool work** — the grip changes with the task (pinch near the blade for fine cuts, fist for
  force); the free hand braces the work; the tool is wiped and returned to its place.
- **Fine motor in cold** — fingers stiff, a mitten off and hanging on its cord, the bare hand works
  a half-beat behind intention, then the mitten goes back on.
- **Wounded hands** — the hand protects the hurt finger, uses the wrist and palm instead.
- **Eating / drinking** — the cup is warm or cold to the hands (they cup it or hold the rim); steam;
  sipping, blowing; food has resistance (tearing bread, chewing).

## Craft processes — show the whole process, it buys belief

A full process on screen is the best-spent time in a film: after it, the audience believes the
character unconditionally. Break the process into visible steps and keep each step's resistance.
Template:

```
PROCESS — [name]: [step 1 with resistance] → [step 2] → [failure/retry] → [the moment it takes] →
[protective/after action]. Hands: [which hand does what]. Tools: [each object and its function].
```

Example (fire by bow drill): a hollow scooped in the crust, grease into the hollow, the kit set —
bow with twisted cord, spindle charred at one end, bone bearing block, hearth board with burnt
sockets and a V-notch, tinder bundle — the drilling, smoke, an ember tipped into the tinder, the
blowing in short breaths, flame. Not "she lights a fire".

## Contact between people

- **Who touches whom is a lock.** State it, and state who nobody touches. The engine invents grabs,
  holds and hugs.
- **Touch has weight** — a heavy hand trembles when it arrives; the receiving head gives slightly
  under the palm.
- **Pushing / shoving** — real sideways momentum; the body carries out of the frame in one motion; the
  feet scramble; hands grab air.
- **Restraint** — fists in the collar from behind; the held body rocks and the holder takes the
  weight.
- **Tenderness** — specific and cultural (nose to cheek and a short inhale; forehead to forehead) —
  name the exact gesture and what it is not ("a father's gesture, no kiss").

## Cold, heat, wet

- **Cold** — breath fog torn by wind the instant it leaves the mouth; frost in brows and lashes; stiff
  fingers; shoulders up; talking through teeth; snow ticking on fabric.
- **Heat** — sweat that darkens fabric at the collar and the back; slow movement; squinting; flies.
- **Wet** — hair stuck to the forehead, water running from cuffs, fabric heavy and darker, shivering
  that comes in waves.

## Objects have a function, not a presence

Every object on a body or in a hand earns its place by what it is for, and the character uses it the
way people who own it do. Name the function in the prompt ("the waterskin is warmed against the body
and steams"), and keep the objects that carry the story locked harder than the rest (hand to
`cineoro-bible` for the project's props master list).
