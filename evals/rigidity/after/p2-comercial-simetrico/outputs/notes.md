## 2. Where the skill pushed me away from the brief
- **Eyes into the lens.** cineoro-performance says the gaze is "off-lens… never into the lens unless the film breaks the fourth wall", and its NOT lists include "looking into the lens". The brief is all four staring into the lens, so the brief wins as a fourth-wall exception. scene-type L also lists "smiling at the lens" as a negative "unless the brief wants a direct address".
- **"Sem expressão" vs living eyes.** The living-eyes toolkit, with saccades, uneven blinks and asymmetry, pushes against the brief's blank deadpan. I kept the living-eyes physiology, kept it minimal and framed the deadpan as a held task. There is a risk that this makes the faces less blank than the user wants.
- **Every face needs a full task ladder.** This pushes a comedy tableau toward psychological depth. I wrote one full block for the father and the short form for the other three.
- **Scene type L (product in use).** It asks for observational 47–63° framing, motivated window or practical light, a person with a real task, and an insert "with information, not decoration". The brief asks for a composed, frontal, high-key look, so I kept only the useful parts: the anti-gloss approach, restating the logo, and the reference for the label.
- **Camera department.** Its default vocabulary is operator body, breathing and "NOT ONE SECOND static", and its catalogue treats a "static locked frame, centred symmetrical composition" as a failure symptom. The brief wants exactly that frame. The style tier allowed it (the authored toolkit), but the language of the department still leans the other way.
- **Light department.** The template opens with "NATURAL / MOTIVATED LIGHT ONLY" and its NOT list bans "bright light, studio light, flat even light". The brief's pastel high-key look needs bright, even, frontal light. I used law 1's "high-key studio box for a commercial" clause.
- **Realism.** The OVER-REAL list, the "If any surface looks clean… WRONG" line, grain, and "realism is what you subtract (darker backgrounds)" all push toward grit. I replaced them with a "material truth of a pristine world" line, which style-and-divergence allows.
- **Sound.** The default policy is diegetic only, with music banned using synonyms. The brief has a jingle, so I described the score instead and did not ban it.
- **Story.** "Start in progress, end hard mid-action" fights the expected commercial ending of a packshot hold. I ended on the packshot but cut it hard mid-bar.

## 3. Rules broken on purpose
- The gaze is into the lens for all four characters, against the performance law. The brief requires it.
- The NEGATIVE does not include "looking into the lens" or "symmetrical/centred". It does include "off-centre framing", which inverts the realist spine.
- There is no handheld or breathing operator and the camera is completely static, against the camera department's operator-body law and its anti-static catalogue.
- The light is flat, even and frontal high-key, which is the light the light department says is "never allowed" in its sourceless form. I gave it a defined source and direction to stay within law 1.
- There is no grain or film stock, and a clean digital look. The FILM LOOK template's realist defaults (halation, heavy moving grain, "MEGA-REAL") were dropped.
- I replaced "OVER-REAL… clean = WRONG" with a pristine-material line.
- The four characters have no identity references, so the CAST is built from text alone. The assets law says "assets first", but this is a one-off 20 s spot and a reference generation would be overkill. I offered Mode E on the card.
- The shot 4 insert is 2 s, at the lower bound of story's "nothing under ~2 s except inserts".
- I did not offer 2–3 directions before locking. This is Mode A, not a project, and I gave one ALTERNATIVE DIRECTION line instead.
